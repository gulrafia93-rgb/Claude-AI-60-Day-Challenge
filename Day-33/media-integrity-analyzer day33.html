Stage 01 · Headline Detective

Would you click this?

The concept

Headlines are written to be clicked, not to be accurate.

Publishers earn attention, and attention pays. A headline's job is often to provoke curiosity or alarm in under two seconds — even if the article underneath tells a calmer, more complicated story. Learning to spot the gap between the headline and the facts is the single most useful media literacy skill you can build, because it's the first thing you see, every time, everywhere.

Stage 02 · Emotion Detector

How did that make you feel?

The concept

Strong feelings are often the product, not a side effect.

Fear, outrage, and tearful nostalgia all make you stop scrolling — and stopping is the whole business model. Posts written to spike a specific emotion often reuse the same small toolkit: urgency, secrecy, scarcity, guilt, or tribal anger. Once you can name the technique, it loses most of its pull on you.
